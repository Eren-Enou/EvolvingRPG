# From combat evidence to a developing character

This is a small implementation path, not a commitment to every proposed progression mechanic. Technical verification and player acceptance are separate gates.

| Boundary | Current implementation | Remaining question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fundamentals and commitment | Existing movement, Light/Heavy, dodge, collision and frozen action snapshots | Player readability and feel across contexts |
| Character experience | Bounded Heavy facts after completed live actions; explicit retention and save migration | Whether full-recovery rules fairly describe experience, including initiative and useful trades |
| Possibility/knowledge | Verified evasive Momentum clues and reveal | Whether players understand the cause without feeling forced into a routine |
| Deliberate identity | Owned Breaker/Momentum choice, separate evolution learning/configuration | Whether the experimental opportunity cost expresses a recognizable identity |
| Durable character | Explicit A/B saves, legal configuration, bounded experience, protected backups | Future migration/content policy and a sustainable reward source |
| Familiarity effect | Older isolated recovery trial remains available; no owned modifier added | A benefit worth deliberately selecting without erasing liabilities |
| Sustained growth | Long-term charter and finite authored evidence boundary | Meaningful repeated growth, retention budget and reward pacing are not solved by first-time facts |

The current history set is the smallest durable bridge. Its presence also spends its lifetime credit budget, so save/reload cannot reopen repeated credit. It supplies no purchase points and does not change the existing discovery rule. Adding an unlimited counter later would need a new policy; the finite history design does not solve that economy automatically.

## 1. Does experience describe competent play fairly?

**Decision:** create an opening with an early Heavy, wait to intercept, or choose Light/movement when Heavy is unsafe. The record should describe what actually happened without encouraging a player to wait for an enemy attack solely to earn progress.

**Builds on:** completed-impact/guard-break/interception facts and the earlier reactive-only practice trial. **Consequence:** Heavy still commits; interruption discards pending experience in this fixture. A worthwhile trade can therefore be excluded, which needs feedback rather than a hidden assumption that every rejected trade was poor play.

**Smallest comparison:** use a fresh Baseline character for an approaching opponent, then a fresh Breaker for a guarded windup. Inspect a completed hit, a missed/interrupted hit and repetition after reset. Compare the factual explanation with the player's explanation of the exchange; no new modifier is needed.

**Support:** legitimate initiative records correctly and players can explain why a fact appeared. **Revise:** completion gating rejects common worthwhile exchanges or the messages imply that fundamentals should be avoided. **Reject as a reward gate:** players feel compelled to perform a checklist. History may remain descriptive without becoming a prerequisite. Prerequisite: player feedback on this playable slice.

**Prepare without Unity:** use the offline history/deduplication fixtures and this completed/miss/interrupted/repeat comparison as the observation sheet. Record intended action, actual outcome, credit explanation and player agreement. **Still requires Unity/player:** live receipts, readable messages, initiative and worthwhile-trade judgment.

## 2. Can familiarity offer a useful deliberate refinement?

**Decision:** whether a modest handling refinement is worth an explicit development choice while keeping the discipline's defining exposure. It must not become a toll paid to repair unreliable controls.

**Builds on:** the existing PracticeTest recovery trial, immutable snapshots and actual recovery review. **Consequence:** reducing base recovery may cross an important enemy timing boundary while leaving miss penalties intact; a universally safer form with no meaningful alternative would be weak specialization design.

**Smallest comparison:** first compare the existing isolated trial off/on against both current opponent roles, recording contact, return to control and the next threat. Do not stack it with Retaliatory Momentum yet. If useful, propose one explicit owned/configured refinement and its real opportunity cost in a subsequent assignment.

**Support:** a recognizable application improves while miss exposure and other fundamentals remain useful. **Revise:** only a number changes perceptibly, or a timing boundary trivializes the opponent. **Reject:** the proposed refinement mainly fixes a baseline defect or is automatically mandatory. Prerequisites: history policy acceptance, benefit validation and a selected investment/configuration policy; history alone does not grant power.

**Prepare without Unity:** specify the off/on comparison with columns for starting distance, contact/miss, return to control, next threat and whether the liability still matters. The existing review exposes recovery; do not fabricate timing observations. **Still requires Unity/player:** run the isolated trial and decide whether its benefit deserves ownership at all.

## 3. Does a developed character approach the same opening differently?

**Decision:** take a longer return, preserve ordinary Momentum, use Light, reposition or leave. A discovery should expand judgment rather than prescribe a combo.

**Builds on:** existing owned Momentum, verified return opportunity, forward intent and action review. **Consequence:** committed travel and linked-target miss recovery remain; contact can leave an unsafe ending position.

**Smallest comparison:** use the same saved character with the effect inactive/active, then vary only the starting distance, an existing obstruction or the existing visible second opponent. Alternate comparison order. Do not compare advanced Momentum to basic Breaker as equal investment.

**Support:** the player can predict the form, sometimes decline it for a clear reason, and recognize the character capability separately from their own improved understanding. **Revise:** forward intent opts in accidentally, or clues/review obscure the actual threat. **Reject a new handling talent:** ordinary intent and better information already solve the observed problem. Prerequisites: player observations from the earlier return probes; no further combat mechanic is selected here.

**Prepare without Unity:** define paired inactive/active observations using the same character and investment, with distance/obstruction/second-opponent variations and reversed trial order. Record predicted form, chosen action and ending exposure. **Still requires Unity/player:** verify intent selection and camera/collision integration, then ask when the player deliberately declines the return.

## What follows these experiments

Only after a refinement or contextual application earns acceptance should its experience prerequisites and spending policy become durable progression. Repeated rewards then need their own replay-resistant budget committed with gains. A mission/hub wrapper can eventually give those choices a session rhythm, but it is explicitly outside this assignment. No current save field promises equipment, missions, account power or infinite reconstructed history.
