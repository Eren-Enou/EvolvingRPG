## The playable promise

Start as an understandable Warrior with useful fundamentals. Enter a dangerous place for a reason. Learn something about how you prefer to fight. Return with a meaningful decision. Invest in that direction, then discover that the same familiar actions can produce a different experience next time.

The short loop is an exchange: read, position, commit, resolve, recover. The session loop is prepare, undertake a mission, adapt, return, choose. The long loop is develop an identity, encounter unfamiliar pressures, discover another possibility, and decide whether it belongs to this character.

These loops must support each other. A mission cannot require grinding an unrevealed talent. A mastery rule cannot reward deliberately prolonging a boring exchange more than completing it. An enemy adaptation cannot make the character's chosen specialty obsolete. Equipment cannot make character investment meaningless.

## Four connected pillars

**Actions remain legible.** Light, Heavy and Dodge do not become a hidden sequence puzzle. Developments change decisions around those inputs. Timing, collision and feedback must agree.

**History opens possibilities.** What happened matters, but a raw action counter does not secretly select a class. A character may learn of a path and decline it. Experiences are interpreted through authored rules and explained after discovery.

**Commitment creates identity.** A character can grow deeply without eventually owning every specialty's peak. Broad fundamentals stay useful, and a supporting hybrid should be possible. The exact commitment and respec rules require explicit comparison.

**The world offers different questions.** Missions, opponents, terrain and local developments create pressures that invite adaptation. The world does not secretly scale to erase the player's recent success.

## Proposed production envelope

The first complete game-shaped slice needs one Warrior, one weapon family, one compact hub, one mission site, a few enemy roles, one culminating encounter, two visible development choices, one contextual discovery, one mastery track and reliable character persistence. It does not need a second class, a procedural continent, an auction economy or infinite generated talent content.

Within that envelope, build the smallest full loop before increasing content quantity. Two missions that generate different decisions are more useful than ten corridors with identical enemies. Two distinct Warriors are more convincing than a large static tree of percentage bonuses.

No duration or content count in these designs is a commitment to a release schedule. A proposed first-hour structure is a pacing hypothesis to test, not a promise that every player progresses on a timer.

## Authority and design changes

The vision and talent charter establish intent. This atlas proposes ways to satisfy it. Existing experiment documents describe bounded prototypes. A document cannot promote its own suggested formula, class name or capacity rule into an established requirement.

Maintain a decision record with: question, alternatives compared, observed result, user decision, affected content, and reversibility. Technical success is recorded separately from player acceptance. Keep rejected prototypes available as design history without continuing to implement their assumptions.

## What a successful early version would demonstrate

- A new player understands enough to fight before reading a talent map.
- Returning to the hub offers a decision that changes the next mission's approach.
- Two Warriors differ in commitment, positioning and opening selection using familiar controls.
- A discovery is reproducible, understandable and optional.
- A failed mission teaches something and preserves clearly communicated progress.
- An enemy adjustment remains readable and leaves a viable response for the chosen specialty.
- Save/reload preserves character history without carrying active combat effects or transferring power to a new character.

These are future evaluation goals. They are not claims about the current prototype.
