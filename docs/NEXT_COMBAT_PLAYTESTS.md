# Three deeper questions for the current combat loop

These are prioritized playtest proposals, not implemented talents or player findings. Keep the current permissive stamina, attack profiles and two opponents. Use separate saved characters for discipline comparisons. Do not compare evolved Momentum against basic Breaker and call that a matched investment result.

## 1. Spend the opening, or preserve the exit?

**Decision:** after a verified evasion, commit to the longer return, use ordinary Momentum, take Light, walk or withdraw. A valid opportunity need not be a command to consume it.

**Foundation:** existing target-linked return, forward-intent opt-in, collision and the optional fast opponent. **Consequence:** the longer approach fixes facing and travel; missing the linked opponent adds recovery. A landed hit can still end in an exposed position.

**Smallest experiment:** compare the same developed Momentum character with the effect inactive/active, first on an open line and then near an existing obstacle. After that, use the existing optional fast opponent as another visible threat. Establish opportunities through real evidence; do not grant an armed flag. Alternate comparison order. Ask the player to predict their ending position and explain a decision to take or decline the return.

**Support:** the extra reach enables a recognizable application in one context, while ordinary actions remain useful in another. **Revise:** travel is hard to predict or forward intent chooses the form unintentionally. **Reject the application:** the longer form is always preferred or never meaningfully useful. A low usage rate alone is not rejection evidence. Improve the cue before inventing another talent.

## 2. Break the guard, then choose what the space is for

**Decision:** a Breaker character can exploit disruption for pressure, a flank, distance or time to address another opponent. The break is an advantage rather than a compulsory combo starter.

**Foundation:** current planted Breaker, ordinary Light, poise, stagger and post-stagger recovery. **Consequence:** preparing the next Breaker keeps the character exposed and gives up movement; a short action or retreat gives up some immediate damage. No guaranteed follow-up or new immunity is proposed.

**Smallest experiment:** observe consecutive Breakers against the guarded role, then repeat with the existing fast role visible. Record actual contact, stagger and return-to-control times. The earlier paper study predicts a possible repeated-break loop; verify it before balancing around it. Also inspect a nearly broken guard where Light may finish disruption sooner than another Heavy.

**Support:** target state or the second threat changes the player's timing or next action while Breaker remains recognizable. **Revise:** repeated breaks erase every decision in both contexts; first inspect existing timings and clarity. **Reject a proposed follow-up talent:** ordinary movement/Light already expresses the useful decision. Do not add an anti-Breaker enemy or nerf stamina as a shortcut.

## 3. A shorter return as an alternative commitment

**Decision:** if extended return proves useful but repeatedly leaves an interesting, poorly expressible close-range choice, compare a selected shorter return against the extended form. This is a candidate handling alternative, not a universal upgrade or an extra modifier key.

**Foundation:** current acceptance-time snapshots and safe-spawn configuration. **Consequence to investigate:** shorter travel gives up reach; it must retain facing commitment, collision and meaningful recovery. It cannot simultaneously gain Breaker's disruption, tracking or free cancellation.

**Smallest future implementation:** one isolated replacement handling form in a comparison scene, with the same resources and clearly displayed travel. No new tree, currency or discovery grind. Do not ship it until question 1 demonstrates the need; neutral ordinary Momentum may already solve the problem.

**Support:** starting distance changes which form is useful and the player can predict the consequence. **Revise:** one form dominates, or selection is mere upkeep. **Reject:** the desired expression is already available through existing intent or the request is really for clearer controls.

## Recording observations

Keep facts and interpretation separate. Applied damage, displacement, interruptions and committed recovery are facts; why a player chose to withdraw is their explanation. Do not award hidden restraint progress for inactivity or infer tactical intent from a missed button press.

For each useful exchange, note character/configuration, opponent, starting context, intended action, actual result and the player's explanation. A small session-only action review can help inspect the facts. It is neither durable mastery nor a reward ledger and must not influence discovery eligibility.

The next implementation choice depends on these observations. Full mastery persistence still requires a durable replay/budget policy; serializing the session-only HeavyPractice points would bypass that decision.
