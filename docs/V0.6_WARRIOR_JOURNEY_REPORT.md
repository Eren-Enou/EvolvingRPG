## Natural route and persistence

On November 10 the focused native relay and live-route suites passed. The scripted Warrior walked from hub spawn to the board, navigated around the barrier, defeated both live opponents through normal combat, activated the relay, and invested its one earned point in Momentum. After explicit save and reload, it ran the full route again. The repeat used the equipped advancing Heavy, earned no additional point, and saved new Heavy experience. The camera followed the player into both zones. The baseline clear received 12 damage; the Momentum repeat received 48 under the same scripted decision policy. These are observations about that policy, not balance comparisons.

Movement used the Starter Assets motor; attacks and dodges used normal acceptance, collision and damage resolution. Board and relay transitions in the route called the same production methods as gameplay interactions. A separate native suite exercised the focused F binding at both stations, menu input ownership, and controlled failure, abandonment, retry, A/B switching and fall recovery. No personal saves or opponent overrides were used in the live clear. The test saves are isolated under ignored `Logs`.

A same-ID reload previously left the settled attempt on the board. `OwnedCharacter` now signals successful record replacement synchronously, so reload clears the previous attempt and a deployment immediately after load remains active. Save schema 3, explicit saves and protected migrations are unchanged. The command-line runner no longer mistakes an earlier report for its own result or passes an invalid stage selection without running anything.

## First expedition presentation

The hub legend identifies the three existing stations and shows a specific F prompt when in range. The board presents objective and reward rules before deployment. The field HUD describes the current objective, both defenders' readable states, health/stamina, the committed attack or recovery, and the last meaningful hit, miss or interruption. Enemy shield, body color and strike guides remain in the scene. Detailed enemy health, poise and experience diagnostics are available through an optional board toggle. The debrief separates the outcome, reward and new observations, with a direct specialization action after the first clear or retry after a failed/abandoned expedition.

All 15 native suites passed with this presentation, including the live route, focused input, ownership and teardown checks (November 10 at 00:24 PST). The offline state harness also passed 11 cases and 9,111 repeated assertions. These establish technical compatibility; the new presentation has not been accepted in human playtesting.

## Contract routes and independently earned builds

Priority Interdiction places the guarded defender in the center lane and the fast fighter east of the relay barrier. The wider east approach can avoid drawing the guard initially. The shorter center approach draws it, letting a player commit to clearing both before extraction. The board explains the choice and the debrief says whether the guard was cleared or bypassed. Restore Relay keeps the original encounter positions and still requires both defenders. Only the relay loop sets contract anchors; shared AI, stamina, attack assets and scene geometry are unchanged. Two new optional Inspector fields on `ExpeditionLoop` tune the Priority patrol anchors; the existing scene needs no setup.

Focused native verification passed at 00:27 PST. All three profiles cleared both contracts using real movement, collision and enemy AI. Baseline and Momentum left the guard standing in Priority; Breaker engaged it via the center. Independently created Warriors each earned one first-restoration point and invested it in a different discipline. Their equipment selection and qualifying Heavy experience survived switching between isolated slots A/B. Against the same Restore Relay guard, the scripted policy accepted five Breaker Heavies and eight Momentum Heavies; the snapshots showed 60 vs. 25 poise, 0 vs. 1.2 m advance and 0.85 vs. 0.45 s base recovery. These observations show different mechanics on the same encounter; the script was not optimized separately for each build and does not establish comparative balance or player preference.

## Test it in Unity

1. Open `Assets/Game/Scenes/RelayOutpost.unity` in the existing Editor. Press Play and click Game view. No scene generation or Input Actions configuration is required.
2. Walk to the gold board and press **F**. Select **Restore Relay**, fight both defenders, go around the center barrier and press **F** beside the blue relay. Try **Q/E**, **Space**, mouse look/zoom, **LMB/RMB** and **Left Shift** during the route.
3. Inspect development, preview and confirm one specialization, and explicitly save in an unused slot. Stop and restart Play, load that slot and repeat the expedition. Confirm your technique survives reload and there is no second completion point. Use only a slot you intend to write.
4. Try defeat or abandon with **F** at the arrival beacon. Check that you return to a usable hub, can retry, and understand which progress stays in memory and what requires an explicit save. Use another empty slot for an independent Warrior.

Automated: **Tools > EvolvingRPG > Run Extended Regression Checks** from one saved, stopped scene. **Run Live Relay Route Check** isolates the walking/fighting/reload route. `tools/RunUnityChecks.ps1 -Stages '13,14'` launches one dedicated interactive Editor when none is open. Do not use it while editing; the simulation supplies input without desktop control. Reports: `Logs/ExtendedChecks.txt`, `Logs/ExpeditionChecks.txt`, `Logs/LiveRelayRouteChecks.txt`.
